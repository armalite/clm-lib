Findings:
- **Neither context mechanism was exercised.** The model solved every held-out task in 3–4 calls with 2–3 targeted searches.
  - Pressure was flagged in 7 runs, always on the final-answer request. In the baseline, only the 4-entry tail existed at that point, so a summary wasn't eligible; in CLM, the model answered instead of editing.
  - This is the outcome SPEC §6 says to report: the agent avoided pressure by reading efficiently. The tasks were not made harder afterwards.
- **No quality difference** can be supported. With score/2 both arms are 6/6. The only score/1 failure is a scorer artifact (next item).
- **Cost.** CLM averaged $0.014 (about 23%) more per run. Part of this is its longer system prompt, about 350 extra tokens per call. The rest is run-to-run variation, including one 4-call CLM run. With n = 6 per arm and no context management happening, this does not show anything about CLM's cost.
- **Scorer correction (post-hoc, disclosed).** In `20261004T065650-summary-heldout-2-f005` the answer was `required_value: "clients.pricing-core.timeout_ms=750"`, which is the correct effective value (truth `750`) in the `setting=value` form used by the authoritative change record. score/1 required the bare value and marked it `wrong`.
  - score/2 also accepts `<identifier>=<value>`, still with stale detection; colon-separated serials are unaffected.
  - It is applied uniformly to all runs by `clm-lib report` from each run's recorded answer and truth. The recorded `evaluator/score.json` files (score/1) were not modified.
  - This change was made after seeing a held-out result. It changes the evaluator, not the agent, prompts or limits, and no runs were repeated.

## Experiment 002: staged incident under sustained context pressure

Full write-up: `experiments/002-staged-incident-context-pressure/README.md`, exported with all evidence to [clm-lib-test-results](https://github.com/armalite/clm-lib-test-results).

- **Calibration** on fresh dev instances, one run per arm:
  - summary, `20261004T080421-summary-staged-dev-1-3ddb`: 4 summaries from stage 2;
  - clm, `20261004T080624-clm-staged-dev-2-c8e9`: 2 unprompted edits at stage 2.
  Both arms managed context with work remaining, so nothing was adjusted. Cost $0.580.
- **Frozen comparison** `20261004T080858`:
  - settings: `configs/default.toml` (identical to 001), HEAD `1230323` plus a saved source patch;
  - tasks `staged-eval-1..3` × 2 modes × 2 repetitions;
  - 12 of 12 cells completed, with none missing, invalid or halted. Cost $3.616.

| arm | strict success | mean cost | mean calls | mean executions | mean output tokens | mean elapsed | management |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

**Measured:**
- CLM was cheaper in all 6 matched pairs and strictly correct in all 6 runs.
- The summary arm failed twice:
  - an explicit `context_overflow`: the verbatim 4-entry tail held a 6,105-char observation;
  - an `unsupported`: a 24-line evidence range broke the published 20-line limit, though the range does contain symptom lines.
- No stale values or stale hypotheses occurred in either arm.
- The stage-1 origin fact was cited by all 11 completed runs.

**Inferred from the traces:**
- The cost gap comes from the baseline's repeated summary calls ($1.026, 46% of its cost). CLM spent *more* on its own action calls ($1.39 vs $1.20) while doing more investigation steps.
- The quality gap comes from the two baseline failure modes above, not from lost early facts.

**Untested:** a stronger baseline (for example a token-bounded tail or incremental summaries), other task families, models or effort levels, and statistical significance (n = 6 per arm).
